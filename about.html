About

A blog about the point where a few things intersect.

I started keeping these notes on my phone in 2019, mostly on a commute I didn't enjoy, mostly because I was tired of reading financial advice written for people who already had $10,000 spare and an advisor named Chad.

Vertex Theory is named after the point where a few different lines cross. For me that's money, technology, and the small decisions that quietly compound into a life — good or bad, whether you're paying attention or not. I write about all three, usually because I'm trying to work something out for myself first and the writing is how I check my own thinking.

A few things I believe, for context:

  • Most financial advice is written for people who don't actually need it.
  • The best technology is the kind you eventually stop noticing.
  • Compounding is the only reliable magic trick in personal finance, and it's boring on purpose — that's kind of the whole trick.

I'm not a licensed advisor, a former hedge fund analyst, or a productivity guru with a 5 a.m. routine you should copy. I'm someone who reads more than is probably healthy, tries things on myself before writing about them, and writes it down so I don't have to relearn the same lesson eighteen months later.

Nothing on this site is financial, legal, or medical advice. It's one person's notes, published in public, made as carefully as I know how.

How this site is built

Vertex Theory runs on plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with Firebase handling the database, login, and analytics underneath. There's no framework, no build step, and no server to maintain — every post you're reading came out of an admin panel on a phone, not a desktop. If that sounds like a strange way to build something, it mostly is, and I'd do a few things differently next time.
